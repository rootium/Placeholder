# How I picked this idea

I wrote down 40 ideas, scored them, and killed 39. Here's the whole trail so
nobody has to take my word for it.

## The filter

Five hard rules. An idea had to pass all five, not four.

1. **It has to run with no server.** No signups, no database, no API keys, no
   monthly bill for me. If it can't be a folder of files on a CDN, it's out.
2. **The output has to be worth sharing.** If a user never shows the result to
   anyone, I pay for every single visitor. If they do, each user brings more.
3. **A tired person has to get it in five seconds.** No tutorial. No empty
   screen. No "create your first project."
4. **It has to be pretty on its own.** Screenshots are the whole marketing
   budget.
5. **Money has to be obvious later.** Not day one, but I need to see the road.

## Why #40 won

Ideas 31 through 39 were all circling the same drain: each is *one good effect*
or *one good layout*. Individually thin. The insight was that they're the same
product cut into pieces.

A risograph print, a halftone poster, a cyanotype, a screenprint — these are the
things people already pay real money for and hang on real walls. The software to
make them is either a $23/month subscription or a stack of Photoshop actions you
have to know how to use. Nobody has made the fast, free, obvious version.

So: **you drop in a photo and get back something that looks like it came off a
printing press.** Not a filter. A finished, framed, print-resolution poster.

It clears every rule:

- **No server.** Every effect is maths on a canvas. Your photo never leaves your
  machine — which is also the single best privacy line in the pitch.
- **Shareable.** The output is a poster. Posters get posted. That's the loop.
- **Instant.** Drop a photo, and eight styles render on *your* image at once.
  You pick one. No blank page, ever.
- **Beautiful.** The screenshots are the product.
- **Money later.** Print-on-demand is the obvious road: you already made the
  artwork, we'll mail it to you framed. Pro pack for extra styles. Neither is
  needed for v1.

## What it's called

**Pressd.** Short, it's a printing press, and it's a thing you did to a photo.

The one-liner: **Drop a photo. Get art.**

## The honest risks

- **The effects have to be genuinely good.** A bad halftone looks like a 2009
  Instagram filter. This is the whole bet, so most of the build went into the
  print engine, not the buttons.
- **Big exports are heavy.** A 300 DPI A3 poster is 20+ million pixels of
  per-pixel maths. Handled by previewing small and rendering large only when you
  hit download.
- **One-visit risk.** Someone makes one poster and leaves. Fought with breadth —
  ten engines, six layouts, seven paper sizes — so there's a reason for visit two.
